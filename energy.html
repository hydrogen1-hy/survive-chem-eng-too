Energy balances

Sensible heat, phase changes, heats of reaction, adiabatic reactor and flame temperatures, and the steady-flow energy balance on a unit. Every result shows the equation with your numbers in it, step by step.

About the data. The built-in heat capacities, heats of formation and latent heats are typical published values (Felder and Rousseau, Elementary Principles of Chemical Processes, Appendix B): check against your course table. Textbooks differ in the last digit. The Data tab lets you type your own values, and every calculation uses them.

Units

These apply to the fields and to the headline answers. The working below each answer is always in °C, kJ/mol, mol/s and kW.

Sensible heat

ΔH = ∫ Cp dT between two temperatures, for one species or a mixture. For a mixture give mole fractions, or flows (only the ratios matter): ΔH = Σ yᵢ ΔHᵢ. For an ideal gas it also gives ΔU = ΔH − R ΔT.

Phase change

The enthalpy change of one species between two states, where the path may cross its melting or boiling point. The path is shown step by step: heat each phase up to the transition, change phase (ΔHfus or ΔHvap), then heat on.

Latent heat at a temperature

ΔHvap at any temperature below the critical temperature by the Watson correlation, ΔHv(T) = ΔHv(Tb) [(1 − T/Tc)/(1 − Tb/Tc)]^0.38, from the value at the normal boiling point. The heat of fusion is tabulated at the melting point.

Heat of reaction

ΔH°rxn at 25 °C from standard heats of formation, ΔH°rxn = Σ νᵢ ΔH°f,ᵢ, and at another temperature by the path: reactants to 25 °C, react, products to the new temperature. Write the reaction with formulas and an arrow, for example CH4 + 2 O2 -> CO2 + 2 H2O. A phase in brackets, such as H2O(g), fixes the phase; without one each species is taken as it exists at 25 °C and 1 atm (liquid water).

Heat of combustion and heating value

Complete combustion to CO₂, H₂O and N₂. The higher heating value (HHV) has the water leave as a liquid; the lower (LHV) as vapour.

Reactor and flame temperature

The outlet temperature from the energy balance Σ ṅout ĥout(T) = Σ ṅin ĥin + Q, with ĥ measured from the elements at 25 °C (heats of formation included). The equation is solved by bracketing and bisection, so it finds the answer without a starting guess, even when a boiling point lies on the way.

Air is 21 mol % O₂ and 79 mol % N₂. Complete combustion of the converted fuel to CO₂ and H₂O. The basis is 1 mol/s of fuel.

Steady-flow energy balance

Q − Ws = ΔḢ + ΔĖk + ΔĖp for streams entering and leaving one unit. Q is the heat added to the unit and Ws the shaft work done by it. Give each stream its temperature and contents; the enthalpy of each is worked out from a stated reference state. If the species change between inlet and outlet (a reaction), heats of formation are used.

Heat capacities

Cp = a + bT + cT² + dT³ in J/(mol·K), with T in the unit shown (°C or K). The table lists the coefficients as plain numbers. To add or replace a row, type the coefficients as they are printed in your table and say which powers of ten the table prints.
