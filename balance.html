Mass balance solver

Draw the flowsheet, enter what you know, and the rest is solved. It does the degrees-of-freedom analysis first, then writes the solution out step by step, the way you would on paper, and puts the answers on your drawing.

1. Components

Use the same basis for every flow: all moles, or all mass. Reactions need moles. The unit (kmol/h, kg/s, ...) is yours; the solver only needs them to be consistent.

2. Draw the flowsheet

Add the units, then join them with streams. A stream from one unit to another connects them. A stream that only enters a unit is a feed; one that only leaves is a product. Streams are named S1, S2, ... automatically, and you can rename anything.

Energy balance (optional)

Each stream gets a temperature and each unit a heat duty Q (heat added, in the unit you choose; negative means heat removed). Stream enthalpies come from heat capacities, latent heats and heats of formation: typical published values (Felder and Rousseau, Appendix B), plus anything you changed on the Energy balances page (Data tab). Check them against your course table.

Species for each component

The energy balance needs to know what each component is. A name that matches a species in the data table (H2O, Water, CH4, ...) is picked automatically; choose the species otherwise. Streams are in the phase their temperature gives (below the boiling point liquid, above it gas) unless you choose a phase for the stream when you select it in the drawing.

3. What you know

Flows, compositions, recoveries, conversions. Each one is an extra equation.
